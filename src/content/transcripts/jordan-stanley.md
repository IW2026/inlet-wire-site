## Cold open

**Jordan Stanley:**
The journey of the artist is so strange because you kind of have to decouple yourself from that external eye in order to actually become the artist that you are. But as I get older now, I'm like, oh, maybe I want to do what I want to do.

## Season 2 introduction

**Eric Chan:**
Welcome to Season 2 of Inlet Wire, your direct line to BC artists. I'm Eric Chan. After 25 episodes exploring BC's indie music scene, we are expanding our conversations to include performers, producers, and the creative minds behind the scenes. And we are kicking things off with musical theatre. My first guest is Jordan Stanley, whose journey took them from Idaho to New York before eventually making Vancouver their home. We talk about finding community, artistic identity and what it means to be yourself. But first, how early did Jordan's love of performing begin?

## Early performances and show choir

**Jordan Stanley:**
Honestly, like, I've been performing since I came out of the womb. My mom would do my makeup when I was a little kid and, like, put me in plays. But also my dad was an educator and part of, like, the student services committee at the college that he worked at. And so he would have me and my older brother (I was, like, four or five years old) go to these, like, student retreats of a bunch of college kids, and, like, my dad facilitating, like, how to be a better leader, up in the wilderness, like in the Idaho wilderness, and he would put us in front of the campfire, and we would do, like, a song and dance, basically. Tell jokes, like do stupid things that we learned throughout the day. So it's honestly, since I can remember, I have been doing all sorts of weird stuff.

**Eric Chan:**
Those early campfire performances were just the beginning. Another moment would spark Jordan's imagination.

**Jordan Stanley:**
In my high school, they had a show choir. And the show choir was the thing. They came into the elementary school, and I remember seeing them do their little dance and spin people around and sing. Like, I was so enraptured by that. And it's such a silly thing, but I was like, that's it, singing and dancing. And so I remember just being like, wow, that's what I want to do. And so then I started doing musicals, did choir in school, started doing show choir. I got into the same show choir that I was obsessed with as a kid, did all sorts of really inappropriate musicals. And then sometime in junior high, high school, I was like, okay, I think I might be good at this.

## New York and Joe Iconis

**Eric Chan:**
And that realization would take Jordan a long way from home.

**Jordan Stanley:**
I went from a tiny town in Idaho and flew to New York and spent 15 years in New York doing basically rock-and-roll theatre. I was so fortunate enough to go to a school that had a professor that had a really, a best friendship with a composer, a musical theatre composer named Joe Iconis, who's kind of like a rock-and-roll mischief maker in the theatre space. And he had just graduated NYU and was like starting to put out all of this stuff. And his whole thing is, like, collecting a collective of people that are kind of, like, very Hedwig and the Angry Inch, this story, like the misfits and the losers of it all, like the strange ones that don't always fit in. I was lucky enough to sort of fall in with them at a really early age. And it was that exact same experience, honestly, now that I'm talking about it. Seeing his shows out at these venues in New York, I was so blown away by the possibility of what this medium could be. Honestly, like, what I could be as a performer. I was like, oh, this is theatre, but also, like, rock and roll and also acting and also, like, relating interpersonally. So it was working with Joe, and then we've done shows together for over 10 years at that point. And all sorts of mischief has been had.

## Finding an artistic voice

**Eric Chan:**
In New York, Jordan found a creative community where being different was something to celebrate. But finding a place to belong was only part of the journey. There was still the question of finding their own voice as an artist.

**Jordan Stanley:**
Being a musical theatre performer, being trained that way, I think I got indoctrinated into this sense of, like, well, I've got to go out and get the job. And, like, once I get the job, then I'll be whatever you want me to be. Very similar to that, like, early memory of, like, oh, well, I'm just who you see me as instead of who I actually feel like I am. Right. But as I get older now, I'm like, oh, maybe I want to do what I want to do. And maybe, like, there are stories that only I can tell. And maybe, like, my artistry is somewhere in there, right? Like, somewhere inside of me, separate from the lens.

## From New York to Vancouver

**Eric Chan:**
For years, New York was home. So how did Jordan end up in Vancouver? As it turns out, that wasn't exactly the plan.

**Jordan Stanley:**
Oh, it's a crazy story. Briefly, COVID, it was 2020. My wife and I were in New York, and theatre shut down. And we spent the summer there, and then my parents are in Idaho. And so we were like, let's go and be outside for the summer, and then we'll come back to New York in the fall. And the fall came and went, and theatre still wasn't happening. And so we were like, well, we've got to, like, perform somewhere. We've got to, like, exercise this artistic energy. And we were doing The Artist's Way at the time. Julia Cameron wrote this book called The Artist's Way that is about unblocking your art, essentially. If you feel like, oh, I know I'm an artist, or maybe I don't even know I'm an artist, but how can I unblock the channels to my creativity in a way that I think really opened my mind up to what I've been talking about. I can be an artist no matter what it is.

**Eric Chan:**
And then an unexpected invitation changed their plans.

**Jordan Stanley:**
We were in Idaho living with my parents, and we were like, oh man, we've really got to do something. And so my wife's family had an empty apartment in downtown Vancouver, and they said, come and stay for six months. And so we did. And as soon as we moved here, we were like, oh, my God, Vancouver is amazing. Like, there's so much beauty. There's so much happening. Film and TV is, like, blowing up here. Voiceover is a huge part of the industry here in Vancouver. And so we really started to settle down. I applied for my permanent residency and then got citizenship last year.

## Identity and rediscovery

**Eric Chan:**
Jordan had found a new home in Vancouver, but leaving New York meant leaving a lot behind.

**Jordan Stanley:**
I was so terrified to move from New York because I felt like I was leaving myself behind. I had identified my person with a location and a job and the group of people that I was with. And moving away, I was like, okay, well, I don't have that or that or that. So who am I? Ultimately, I kind of got given this amazing gift, which is to rediscover yourself as the person you are at this exact moment. And that was mind-blowing for me. Because all of a sudden I was like, oh yeah, I'm gender-fluid. Okay, that's a huge one. I've always known that something was different about me, specifically growing up in a really conservative area of the world. Oh my God, this is it, this is the thing, now I can put my finger on it. That gave me a huge sense of, like, oh, you have always been like this and this and this and this. What does that mean about, you know, who you are? Okay, what if that is a huge part of your strength and identity and artistic fire?

**Eric Chan:**
After years in New York, Jordan discovered something different about Vancouver's theatre scene.

## Vancouver's theatre scene

**Jordan Stanley:**
What was so funny was realizing how much of a small community the theatre scene here is in Vancouver and how intimate and personable it all is. It was so surprising because in New York, you know, you do auditions, and you go in and you sing your song and you walk out and the casting director or whatever says like, okay, thanks. And that's like all you get. And here in Vancouver, we, like, did our first auditions. We came in and they were like, hey, have a seat. And we were like, wait, what? And they're like, so tell us about yourself. And I was like, hi, I'm Jordan. And so it was so strange to have them, like, actually be interested in me, to want to get to know me. I think that was really, really impactful for us, made us feel super welcome.

## Hedwig and the Angry Inch

**Eric Chan:**
And that brings us to Hedwig. Jordan is taking on the lead role in Zee Zee Theatre's Hedwig and the Angry Inch. But their connection to the show goes way back.

**Jordan Stanley:**
I think in my early 20s, I auditioned to be Hedwig at a different theatre in the States and didn't get it, but was called back and was like, what is this show that is exactly what I was just describing, right? Like a super personable, like, audience interaction, like one-to-one kind of improv stand-up show, but about identity, love, heartbreak, you know, rock and roll, all of this, stuff that I really, really identified with. And I was like, how have I not heard of this before, honestly? So when the opportunity came up here, I was just so thrilled. Like, honestly, I couldn't believe it. And for a little bit I really was like, oh my God, like, do I really want to do this? I recognize what a huge privilege and responsibility this role is because it means so much to the queer and trans community. And so taking that on, right, is a huge responsibility.

## Preparing to become Hedwig

**Eric Chan:**
So how does Jordan prepare to become Hedwig?

**Jordan Stanley:**
Oh my God. Yeah, a lot of rest. I would say preparation is a huge part of it, right? It's like knowing the story well enough that you're able to let go of the story a little bit. Okay, I go here and here. This song is like this. And then this moment is actually like very quiet and small. So, like, I've got to make a roadmap of that before I can sort of actually put my human self into that place. And then I think the answer to your question is like, man, I just get on the roller coaster and it takes me. I know that I am prepared, that I am well equipped. I have done all the prep. Now I let all of that go. I literally, the lyrics of the song, I put on some makeup, you know, I turn up the 8-track, I put on my playlist, I put the wig on, and I become her. And in becoming her, you sort of take on this mantle of this, like, superpower of, like, okay, I am that bitch.

## The meaning of Hedwig

**Eric Chan:**
But beyond the performance, there's something deeper in Hedwig's story.

**Jordan Stanley:**
I think what is so meaningful about Hedwig's journey is that ultimately she realizes that the answers were inside herself the entire time. My hope is that audience members leave the show being like, oh my God, it is okay to be myself. You know what? I'm getting emotional because that's, like, so powerful. It's so hard to feel, I think, for people in this, like, very lonely world, but it's so simple. And so I hope through this massively complicated, huge beast of a show, they can realize maybe a simple truth, you know, I am enough.
